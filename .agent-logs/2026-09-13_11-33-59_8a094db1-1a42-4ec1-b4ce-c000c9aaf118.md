## 2. The design — what I'm arguing

Three ideas, in priority order:

**Cards carry a verdict, not a filename.** The blurb is the one sentence you'd want if you never opened the call. The talk-time ribbon plus *"Ana led 31% · 2 people never spoke"* is decision-grade information you currently have to open the call to reach — and it's exactly the eight-person-hour-long case the brief says actually matters. That line is the most defensible pixel on the page.

**Every state of a call is a card.** Live (animated waveform), processing (explicit checklist: recording saved ✓ / transcript ready ✓ / writing summary…), and done. Same object, three moments. The library never goes blank, and the post-call wait becomes legible instead of a missing row — which turns gap #1 above from a bug into a visible, honest state.

**The splash earns its 2.6 seconds** by being a recap, not a loading screen — your week's three numbers while the mark animates. First visit per session via `sessionStorage`, any key skips, and it's removed entirely under `prefers-reduced-motion`. A splash that shows nothing is a tax; one that answers "what did I miss" is the product.

⌘K and the theme toggle are both wired — the palette shows cross-meeting search hits with speaker + timestamp + an "Ask across all calls" row. Token names match your existing `globals.css`, so porting is mostly find-and-replace rather than a rewrite.
